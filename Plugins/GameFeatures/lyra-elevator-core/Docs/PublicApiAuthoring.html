Elevator authoring API

Entity starters and replacement targets

Wizard recipes are contributed by the owner plugin and create content inside the new mod. Enemy and Boss inherit a mod-owned guard Blueprint generated by the canonical Atlas NPC authoring library: perception, navigation, StateTree, GAS attack and health are editable. The combat starter creates four NPC assets. The optional Content Browser command Create Guard and Worker Examples creates the expanded demonstration map, training target and workstation. Enemy registers a stable archetype through Add Enemy Content. Both have a mod-owned item and DeathLoot container; the native loot owner caches generation and consumes only successfully delivered units. Never force a new roll or ignore the remaining amount to retry delivery. Both include the existing Health Save State projection: living health bases restore without damage/death events; a dead actor becomes a tombstone and is omitted on load. Active effects and AI life state have separate owners. NPC snapshot/persistence-pause APIs are available for mods that retain AI memory and resumable routines; the guard starter does not automatically persist every AI decision or active gameplay effect.

Boss uses RoBossEncounterComponent and its mod-owned DA_ExampleBossEncounter: start only from NotStarted, submit health samples to the definition's half-health transition, select the movement profile's Sprint speed in phase Two (including after restore), and finish on actual server NPC death. Only a successful live finish requests its trophy drop. Restored or replicated state never grants loot. The example supplies save participants for encounter/loot and health state. Pickup/world identity and the host save group must be included in a tested round trip before publishing.

Gastronom mannequins

Use Gastronom.Mannequin.Appearance to add a baked limb profile for a shop display. Use the placement tag World.Actor.Slot.Mannequin.Enemy to add a concealed enemy. Both targets and the DLC dependency are documented in the mannequin authoring guide.

First enemy: change one hit

  1. Create an Enemy mod. Open EntityExample/NPC/NPC_ExampleGuard. Its Attacks entry selects GA_ExampleGuardAttack; this entry owns reach and cooldown. Its StateTree selects when to engage. BP_ExampleGuard is the NPC pawn and BP_ExampleEnemy adds the mod identity, persistence and loot. DA_ExampleEnemy registers that concrete pawn.
  2. Open GA_ExampleGuardAttack, locate Apply Combat Damage, and change Damage from 10 to 15. Target Knockback Speed defaults to 150 cm/s for the strike. The node uses the project's damage model, evaluates target policy and resistance, and supplies the hit to feedback. Run once with an ordinary target and once with an immune target. Compare actual health changes; the base damage value is an input.
  3. For a shot or damage over time, choose Add → Atlas → Create Ranged and Periodic Attacks in the NPC folder. Assign one generated ability to the same attack entry, then set the desired range and cooldown. The shot traces visibility and applies damage only on hitting the selected target. The periodic ability captures its initial target, applies three pulses through the same combat system, and ends with the GAS task.
  4. FloorExample/BP_FloorLayout → ExamplePlacement places your enemy on the starter floor. To affect an existing floor, add a placement or an eligible contribution to that floor through its owning placement/prank rules. Registering the archetype alone cannot choose a room or a spawn point.
  5. In the content authoring panel select the mod's GameFeatureData and a saved, playable multiplayer test map with health-enabled player pawns. Click Test Enemy on Client. For a feature with several enemies, select its archetype in the Content Browser first. The separate editor mounts the local source assets for this test, spawns the selected pawn on the server and submits a sight observation of the remote player. The report in Saved/AtlasAuthoring/enemy-…/result.json records the attributed hit, server/client health, AI visibility, intent, distance, culture and observed presentation. Audio asset selection and audible playback are separate checks. Repeat with the player's language and controller settings and inspect the client HUD.
  6. After a released change to the enemy's saved gameplay contract, review GameplayRevision on the archetype and run the save/restore scenario. Cosmetic adjustments alone do not establish incompatible behavior.

Boss encounter definitions

Assign a RoBossEncounterDefinition to the encounter component before starting or restoring the actor. Give it a stable, mod-namespaced DefinitionId, positive GameplayRevision, an InitialPhase and unique phase rows. Each row has ordered signal/value transitions: Any, AtMost or AtLeast; the first matching transition wins. Running selects another declared phase; Completed, Failed or Cancelled requires an empty target phase. The component snapshots the definition per encounter instance. Editing the source asset does not change an already configured instance. Health, animation and arena capabilities supply SubmitSignal calls on authority; the encounter does not poll or own those systems.

EnterActions and ExitActions are semantic requests through the authority-only OnPhaseAction delegate. Bind handlers to existing capabilities for spawning, animation, doors and other mechanics. Context.ActionPhase identifies the phase being exited or entered; Context.State is the accepted new state. A missing handler performs no operation. Use OnEncounterStateChanged to derive persistent presentation, including after load and replication. Restore and binding never replay one-shot phase actions or grant rewards.

An action's empty TargetSlot means the encounter owner. Named slots must be declared in ArenaSlots and bound explicitly with BindArenaSlot to live actors in the same world. A running encounter cannot replace a live target; a destroyed target can be rebound. Arena placement and save systems still own actor creation, identity and restoration; rebind references after restoring those actors. Missing required action targets reject a transition before state changes. The list is a contract for references, not a second arena spawner or persistence system.

Save records retain the definition ID and gameplay revision. Keep these compatible for existing saves; change the revision when changing the saved phase contract. A mismatch fails restoration. AcceptLegacySavedState is an explicit migration opt-in for verified legacy phase compatibility, not a general recovery switch. Unconfigured encounter components retain the manual phase API. A bound participant observes its controller's definition and cannot evaluate transitions or execute its action lists independently.

KLETKA's Collegium, MechanicalFly, Prophetron, Rustbucket and MotherOfConcrete controllers use definitions for their existing health thresholds (50%, 70%, 70%, 65%, 50%). The first four retain their 95% debug threshold through a separate signal. Their bespoke arena and phase handlers remain in Blueprint; limb and cinematic conditions of other bosses retain their authored logic. Extending these definitions does not automatically replace those handlers.

Familiar claims the first overlapping player on authority, retains that PlayerState and interpolates toward its current pawn's follow target. Later overlaps cannot steal ownership. The actor replicates movement. Its Save State and familiar component retain the player's stable save GUID and formation offset, reserve ownership while that player is absent, and rebind on reconnect. The player must have a ready stable save identity; an assigned transient player refuses capture. World Actor is the simple visible placement starter.

Curse creates a server-only on-spawn ability that applies 10 GAS damage once to each current player pawn with an ability system, then ends. Its prank definition and floor contribution are registered together. Resource creates a definition, pack and spawn processor referenced by the floor's loot fragment. Template graph compilation is not packaged/network acceptance; test each authored mod through cook, host/client, late join, restore and deactivation.

Catalogue contributions use stable definition IDs. Add introduces a definition; Replace targets a published existing ID; Remove suppresses a future choice where the owner permits it. Preserve released rule identities and declare the owner dependency. Equal-ranked conflicting replacements fail; activation order does not decide the winner. Existing instances and saved selections keep their accepted implementation instead of resolving again.

The published catalogues now also include Inventory Item and Crafting Recipe IDs. Their owner accepts Target Id in an explicit Inventory Content edit, so the external ModKit does not need a copy of the game's original item/recipe asset. Catalogues contain product-owned definitions only; local test plugins and other mod authors' assets are excluded from the exported game catalogue. An empty recipe catalogue means no product recipe was published, not permission to invent an ID.

For mod-owned progression, use the selected Atlas Progression schema/component and optional Progression Save State adapter with Save Game Savable. See that owner's PUBLIC_API for readiness, schema migration and transaction replay limits. This does not mutate account entitlements or platform achievement state.

For a custom persistent ability-system owner, attach Ro Perk Selection Component before declaring save readiness. Restoring a selected perk must reuse that component: save callbacks cannot add or remove actor components. The game's PlayerState already provides it. The component stores replicated selection metadata; GAS still owns ability grants and effects.

World placements use Add World Actor Rules with an exact placement ID or semantic tag query. Permissions belong to the target point.

For ordinary vending slots, add a Weighted Variant rule to your Game Feature Data. Set Target Tags to match World.Actor.Slot.Vending.Beer, choose your replicated actor class, and set a positive integer Weight. The origin of the slot is at floor height. Adjust the rule's local offset if your actor's origin is above its feet. When the authored location passes its difficulty-adjusted spawn roll, the server selects one class from the base actor and the active contributions. ElevatorCore supplies weights of 70 for live beer (respawn), 3 for Dead Beer and 27 for the bottle bank. A mod that adds one variant of weight 25 gets 25/125 of populated points. An explicit Replace or Remove takes priority. Saved slots keep their selected class. Boss respawn machines use a separate guaranteed placement.

Breakable container points accept Weighted Variant rules targeting World.Actor.Slot.Destructible.Crate, World.Actor.Slot.Destructible.Vase, or World.Actor.Slot.Destructible.WoodenBarrel. Each point keeps its authored spawn chance. At a populated point the default container has weight 100. A mod with weight 25 supplies one in five of the populated points when it is the only contributor. The floor checkpoint records the selected variant. The container owns its mesh, health and loot. Use the loot system when only its contents change.

Target floor lockers, refrigerators and safes with World.Actor.Slot.Container.Locker, World.Actor.Slot.Container.Fridge and World.Actor.Slot.Container.Safe. Locker and refrigerator points have a base object at every authored location. Safe points retain their original spawn chance. At all three, a weighted variant competes with a base weight of 100. The selected class supplies its own door, lock, interaction options and loot. The checkpoint records the choice of class when the floor loads.

Required vehicle targets are narrower:

Get Player Vehicle Target: Rustbucket Replicated Atlas Gameplay Vehicle Pawn; Replace with identity offset
Get Scenery Vehicle Target: VAZ corridor Replicated Actor implementing Le Scenery Vehicle Control; Replace with identity offset

The VAZ interface receives Set Scenery Vehicle Active(Active) on the server. The base B_Jiguli uses its original ToggleActive through the game's compatibility adapter. The controller owns event timing. This scenery contract does not provide player driving; use the public vehicle composition for a drivable car. Do not copy or inherit private controllers.

Required vehicle choices reject Add, Remove and non-identity offsets because their controllers need one compatible car at the authored location. General placement support does not imply that every required gameplay object may be removed. Cooking, saves and multiplayer still require acceptance checks before publishing the mod.

Floor authoring belongs to ElevatorCoreContent inside the existing ElevatorCore plugin. Declare ElevatorCoreContent as a direct module dependency and use the semantic include paths below. Reflected floor types moved from /Script/ElevatorCoreRuntime to /Script/ElevatorCoreContent; the project carries class, struct, enum and delegate redirects.

ElevatorCore-owned types use the Le prefix. Their editor names and categories must identify ElevatorCore rather than use the Atlas prefix reserved for reusable Atlas systems. References to those shared systems retain their original Atlas names.

Inspect a floor in the editor

Open your floor map. In the floor definition Details, choose Inspect open floor map for a report in the Output Log. With the KLETKA Development runtime present, Run seeded floor save and reload launches a separate process for the selected definition and seed. In the standalone SDK the command below the button can be copied for a matching development game.

To inspect by hand, select Python in the Output Log. The following reads the open map and prints the authored floor origins, player starts, navigation bounds and placement points with their tags and readiness flags.

import json, runpy, unreal
from pathlib import Path
recipe = Path(unreal.Paths.convert_relative_path_to_full(unreal.PluginBlueprintLibrary.get_plugin_base_dir("ElevatorCore"))) / "Docs/Examples/CreateFloorModExample.py"
print(json.dumps(runpy.run_path(str(recipe))["inspect_open_floor"](), indent=2))

Open the floor's Game Feature Data and its floor contribution preview to inspect slot weights, session switches and excluded bundles. Choose the host's generation profile to include its base candidates. In the game project, enable Debug Floor Generation on its floor manager to trace the selected slot, bundle and floor for an actual host run. The floor layout origin is the transform at which KLETKA creates its floor controller. A streamed floor can have no local Player Start.

For a mounted floor in the KLETKA development project, start a separate standalone process on the empty Entry map. Supply an absolute fresh test user directory. Substitute your own floor asset path, seed, project and engine paths.

& $EditorCmd $Project /Engine/Maps/Entry?game=/Script/Engine.GameModeBase -game -unattended -nullrhi -nosound "-UserDir=$TestUserDir" '-ExecCmds=Atlas.ModKit.PreviewFloor /MyMod/FloorExample/DA_Floor.DA_Floor 29458'

The process records its result under $TestUserDir/FloorPreview/<run>/result.json and exits. It checks the streamed floor and its checkpoint; mounting and installation of the package are covered by the separate release case.

For packaged Development acceptance, select the floor's cooked package and definition with the floor case of Tests/ModKit/VerifyPackagedContent.py. Pass --floor-seed 29458 (or another nonnegative int32). The case streams the floor on authority, commits a private Atlas run fragment, unloads the level, reopens that slot and streams the restored definition. Its report names the seed, persistent scene ID, controller origin and navigation readiness before and after reload. Run this command in the integration checkout with the matching packaged Development game and cooked packages. The test uses its own user directory.

Test a phased boss, familiar and returning mod save

The capability-save integration case writes a living boss in phase two, an enemy death and an owned familiar. A second process checks the saved health, phase, rank, balance and enemy tombstone. It checks for repeated drops, kills the familiar owner's pawn, observes the unpossessed interval and possesses a replacement pawn with the same player. It then finishes the restored boss and checks that repeated damage after death cannot issue another reward. Readers discard this session. The harness reopens the original bytes again to detect accidental writes.

Use the save case for a fragment with three cooked schema versions. It writes v1, migrates to v2, reopens and commits with the installed mod disabled, re-enables it, then repeats with its package removed and reinstalled. The result records the slot, package ID, unavailable fragment ID and its class path. The v3 negative fixture must reject the migration while preserving the previous bytes. Use your own cooked save fixtures when changing fragment identity or migration behavior.

Floor definition and focused fragments

#include "Core/TerritorySystem/Definitions/LeFloorDefinition.h"
#include "Core/TerritorySystem/Fragments/LeFloorSpawnFragment.h"
#include "Core/TerritorySystem/Fragments/LeFloorAvailabilityFragment.h"

// Called by the owning authoring code, with an appropriate asset/package Outer.
ULeFloorDataAsset* Floor = NewObject<ULeFloorDataAsset>(Outer);
ULeFloorSpawnFragment* Spawn = NewObject<ULeFloorSpawnFragment>(Floor);
Spawn->SpawnChance = 35.0f;
Floor->Fragments.Add(Spawn);

The example creates in-memory data; saving an asset, authoring its Level, and registering a mod contribution are separate owner operations. It does not activate gameplay. Each exact fragment class is allowed at most once; use the existing validation.

Additional focused headers:

  • Core/TerritorySystem/Fragments/LeFloorGenerationFragment.h
  • Core/TerritorySystem/Fragments/LeFloorPresentationFragment.h
  • Core/TerritorySystem/LeFloorRole.h
  • Core/QuestSystem/LeQuestFloorCandidate.h

LeFloorRole declares ELeFloorType without generated runtime/save data. LeQuestFloorCandidate refers to the canonical Atlas quest definition and published revision. FLeFloorSelectionWeight is owned by floor content; shared loot coefficients are FAtlasLootSpawnAdjustmentsData and FAtlasLootSpawnMultipliersData in AtlasLootSystem. The former Roguelike structs redirect to these actual owners; there are no duplicate DTOs.

LeFloorBuiltInFragments.h contains the loot, event and quest fragments. Resource and event selections use stable primary asset IDs. The execution classes and LeFloorTypes.h save state remain private to the game. Serialized legacy object fields are retained as read-only UPrimaryDataAsset references for loading old data; new authoring uses fragments. A present fragment, including an empty one, overrides its legacy pool. Runtime resolves and type-checks the executable event classes in ULeFloorPrankManager::GetConfiguredPranks.

Pawn presentation

This section describes internal game consumers, outside the distributed floor SDK.

Use Experience/LePawnPresentationFragment.h when authoring a presentation fragment on UAtlasGameplayPawnDefinition. Gameplay-only code includes Experience/LePawnProfile.h without the presentation declaration. The existing internal pawn data consumer now includes presentation explicitly.

The old profile's DefaultReticle remains the same serialized soft class property and the existing fallback/preload code remains active. It is read-only in new authoring details. It has not been deleted, type-converted or given a deprecated serialization flag. Existing assets must be migrated and verified before removal.

UMG is private to ElevatorCoreRuntime's Build.cs. If a consumer calls UUserWidget methods or derives a widget, that consumer declares UMG itself. The old compatibility field still names UUserWidget through a forward declaration. No assertion is made that all transitive UI dependency through Ro disappeared.

Public entry policy

Config/ElevatorCorePublicApi.json classifies every source header:

  • ElevatorCoreContent owns the canonical semantic headers, without Runtime forwarders;
  • ElevatorCoreRuntime/Contracts preserves reviewed include entries for internal game consumers;
  • runtime startup and selection helpers remain private.

Floor headers live in their canonical content module and retain their semantic include paths. Their reflected class, struct and enum ownership is redirected from ElevatorCoreRuntime to ElevatorCoreContent by the game's configuration. Do not include private execution headers.

Config/ModKitDistribution.json selects only ElevatorCoreContent and ElevatorCoreContentEditor from the plugin. Private runtime, migration, economy tooling and gameplay assets are excluded. Staging projects the descriptor as an authoring host without activating KLETKA's GameFeatureData or executing its prebuild commands. Each distribution still requires its current build, consumer and editor receipts before publication.

ElevatorCorePublicApiTests compiles every reviewed content header separately and together, without PCH, unity or private include paths. Its only direct dependencies are Core, CoreUObject, Engine and ElevatorCoreContent. Internal pawn/enemy fixtures belong to ElevatorCoreTests.

Test-ElevatorPublicApi.py checks the explicit entry inventory, forwarding targets, narrow local includes, private UMG disposition and consumer coverage. Reusable plugin CI invokes it under ProductLyraBoundary. This static check does not replace UHT, compiling consumers individually, Blueprint loading/resave or multiplayer runtime checks.

Blueprint contribution recipe and acceptance

  1. Author the floor asset and its focused fragments. Validate the definition before registering it.
  2. On the owning GameFeatureData, add Add Floor Content and populate Floors. Add Bundles/Biomes only for content this feature owns; use SlotBundles when contributing a bundle to an explicit FloorFlow slot. Prank references must identify registered Prank primary assets; arbitrary data assets are rejected. Creating new private prank execution classes is outside this SDK surface.
  3. Activate through the feature lifecycle. The action stores its registration handle; deactivation releases that contribution. The author does not call manager filtering steps or mutate a global selection list.
  4. Verify activation, a rejected/invalid contribution and deactivation cleanup in a rebuilt Editor. In-memory native examples and static include checks do not certify that asset flow.

Config/FoundationApiSurface.json assigns a role and owner recipe to the source headers and exact presentation rules to the reviewed manager methods.

A map without private gameplay parents

In an Actor Blueprint, use Add Component → Le Floor Layout. This is the ElevatorCore scene component ULeFloorLayoutComponent, not a separate Data Asset. The generated ModKit includes /AtlasFloorExample/FloorExample/BP_FloorLayout and /AtlasFloorExample/FloorExample/L_ExampleFloor as a working authoring example.

Place exactly one Le Floor Layout (ULeFloorLayoutComponent) on an ordinary Actor in the floor map. Its world transform defines the execution origin. Keep the component in cooked content and provide navigation bounds for the walkable floor. The server creates the game's configured controller in that same streamed level, with replication independent of player distance. The existing game owns navigation, loot, quests, initialization and unload. Clients receive that controller; a mod must not spawn another one.

Existing game maps with an authored controller retain that controller. A component-authored map with no layout or multiple layouts fails loading with the map path and component count. The public SDK does not include the private controller's Blueprint or native parent.

CreateFloorModExample.py generates a fresh Game Feature, an Actor Blueprint with the component, a map with ground and navigation bounds, a floor, bundle, biome and a canonical Quest Blueprint. The action contributes its bundle to Floor.Flow.Slot.Endless. Public slot tags are registered by ElevatorCoreContent, so the authoring project does not require KLETKA's private tag configuration.

Run the recipe in a fresh unattended editor on /Engine/Maps/Entry; it creates an isolated map and preserves the current editor world. The separate reload verifier opens the new map. Existing plugin destinations are rejected. The starter quest completes on activation; add objectives in the Quest Flow editor. Create/reload acceptance checks saved composition and map geometry. It does not by itself certify cooking, multiplayer or save compatibility; those require the packaged product route.

New definitions appear in the editor catalogue before their Game Feature is activated. The catalogue reads their saved primary IDs without changing runtime registration. Resource and curse starters declare their definitions in Game Feature Data's Primary Asset Types to Scan; retain these entries when extending the examples. A renamed or moved definition keeps its generated ID. Duplicating a definition creates a new ID; copying a shipped ID into an unrelated definition is an error.

Create public example maps in the generated ModKit. Their WorldSettings class is /Script/Engine.WorldSettings; the static FloorLayout component is the actor root. The recipe rejects private game world settings, including AtlasWorldSettings.

Cooked delivery

Use the ModKit Package Mod workflow with the installed game selected. It matches the packaged game executable and asset registry to exactly one delivered cooking base under Releases; a similarly named or older base is not interchangeable. Cooking metadata does not include the base game's building assets.

For CI, Tools/CookContentMod.py runs the engine DLC cooker and IoStore writer against that matching base. Its --help lists the project, plugin, engine, release and output arguments. NativeSdk supports C++ editor consumers; packaged game mods use content, Blueprint and Lua. A new native gameplay DLL or class is not delivered by this route.

Distribute the resulting Package directory with its ams_manifest.json, ams_cooked.json, descriptor and containers intact. AMS verifies the container hashes and the receiving game's base registry before mounting. A mod cooked for another game baseline must be cooked again against the matching release metadata. The publisher must produce new immutable cooking metadata with each changed base release and rebuild both public distributions, NativeSdk and BlueprintBinary, from their allow-list.

Feature actions may activate and deactivate between sessions. Mounted containers and loaded package metadata remain resident for the process lifetime; replacing an already mounted mod version requires restarting the application.

Persistent objects in repeated scenes

Add Atlas Savable to an actor that needs persistence and select the host's world persistence group. For an actor authored inside a repeatable floor or generated room, enable Instance Scene Identity, then save the source map to bake its local Persistent Id. Do not assign new IDs during BeginPlay. Duplicate local IDs in one level are errors; identical local IDs in two instances of that level are expected. Existing globally identified actors leave this option off.

The floor manager combines the saved floor identity with each authored actor identity before registration. A generation backend which creates additional streamed levels implements Get Scene Local Id: every actor in one generated room returns the same stable room GUID, and repeated rooms return different GUIDs. The ID must be reproduced when the saved floor is rebuilt. An unrelated actor returns an invalid GUID. Compare Actor.GetLevel with your streaming level's GetLoadedLevel; do not infer identity from transient actor names or allocate random GUIDs inside the query. The built-in Dungeon Architect backend uses its generated chunk ID.

The save registry rejects missing/unprepared identities and duplicate local IDs rather than silently merging records. Rebinding a live level to another floor or room is not supported. Changing the authored identities of a released map requires an explicit migration of its saves.

For a disposable trap, enable One-Shot Trap on Trap State in addition to Atlas Savable. Its first accepted trigger consumes the actor for subsequent loads, even if a delayed effect is still running when state is collected. Loading omits the actor and never restarts that effect. Reusable traps leave the flag off. These components only collect state: an explicit Save call is still required to commit a checkpoint to disk.

Device, progression and presentation starters

The New Mod wizard also offers these owner recipes:

  • Persistent cage device addition creates a movable replicated actor and an Add rule for the published scanner target. Its Actor Owner is the elevator before BeginPlay. The host attaches and persists it through its existing world placement/save owners. The floor map is an isolated actor preview; validating attachment requires the real game cage.
  • Saved progression training station owns one instance subject, a schema and the optional Progression Save State adapter. A new subject receives 10 points; each player overlap buys a rank for 3 points, capped at three ranks. Purchases use the observed record revision. Restored records must not be reinitialized or funded again. This is actor-owned progression, not player account statistics or platform achievements.
  • Respawn purchase rules binds one public policy component to GameState. Its asset controls purchase eligibility and price. Custom policies may select a subset of game-provided recipients; they cannot admit otherwise ineligible players or mutate an accepted receipt.
  • Spatial subtitles creates an emitter, localized text constant, mod-owned style and a scoped display in HUD.Slot.ModeStatus. A local player overlap emits a four-second caption. Add Widgets removes its own HUD contribution on feature deactivation. A different host must provide that slot or explicitly bind another one. Existing global subtitle/media producers are not migrated by this example; retain only the displays appropriate for your host UI.

Game Feature creation, saved owner wiring and runtime behavior are separate checks. The external acceptance suite creates and reloads the recipes, including the editable main-menu screen; that alone does not certify packaged host/client behavior, late join, save restoration or mod updates. The current task receipts record which of those checks actually completed.
